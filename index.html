Reading · Expository Blog Post

Expository

Voluntary work means you do the work by choice to help others without getting paid.

My Experience at the Cooperative Society

A blog post by Jassim · Volunteer · Kuwait

Last weekend, I decided to volunteer at our local cooperative society (co-op). I wanted to be a useful resident in my neighbourhood and learn how community projects really work.

When I arrived, the manager welcomed me and explained the daily tasks. We helped put together food packages for the elderly who could not leave their homes. People donated rice, sugar, oil and dates as a contribution to families in need. The volunteers also checked the prices of the goods to make sure they were fair for every customer.

During the pandemic, the co-op had a huge impact on our community. It delivered medicine and food to people who had to stay at home. My job was small, but I gladly carried boxes, wrote names on the lists, and called families to confirm deliveries. I tried to demonstrate a positive attitude so others would feel encouraged.

It is necessary and vital for young people to support charity and volunteering. My grandfather persuaded me to keep going, and I realised something important: a community is only strong when everyone establishes the habit of helping others.

I left the co-op feeling proud. I learnt that small actions can change big things — and I will be back next weekend. Will you join me?

Lesson 2

The First Conditional

Grammar · Real future possibilities

📘 The Rule

We use the first conditional to talk about real or possible future results that depend on a present condition.

If + present simple → will + base verb
  • If volunteers help, the beach will be cleaner.
  • If nobody cleans the beach, it will look dirty.
  • The campaign will be successful if people join it.
💡 Add a comma only when the "if" clause comes first.
⚠️ Do not use "will" in the if-clause: If people will help… ✓ If people help…

Small Acts, Big Impact

Listening · Conversation in the diwaniya

Before You Listen

  • What does being a good citizen mean to you?
  • Can small acts of kindness make a difference? Give examples.
  • How do responsible people help their neighbourhood?

🎧 Listen to the conversation

Ali and Salman talk in the diwaniya about Community and Volunteering.

Track 5.1 — Small Acts, Big Impact Browser text-to-speech
Show transcript

Salman: Hi Ali! I have been thinking — what does it really mean to be a good citizen?

Ali: Good question, Salman. For me, it means doing small things every day to help others. Volunteering, when you can, makes the community stronger and more connected.

Salman: Like joining a community clean-up?

Ali: Exactly. Picking up litter is a small act, but it has a big impact. The streets become safer and cleaner for everyone.

Salman: What about helping younger students at school?

Ali: Yes! When we help younger students, we teach them respect and honesty. That is how values pass from one generation to the next.

Salman: So even being polite to neighbours counts?

Ali: Absolutely. Greeting your neighbours, helping the elderly carry their bags, or visiting a sick friend — these small acts build trust. Honestly, I love volunteering. It makes me feel useful and proud of my community.

Salman: I will start with our school garden project this week!

Ali: Great! If everyone gives a little time, our community will become a better place for all.

Lesson 5

Leading Humanitarian Institutions in Kuwait

Reading · Expository Article

Think · Pair · Share

  • Why do countries create organisations to help people in need?
  • Which charitable organisations in Kuwait have you heard of? What do they do?
  • How do volunteers make their community stronger?

Kuwait is one of the leading Gulf countries that promotes voluntary work.

Kuwait is known for its generous spirit and care for others. Many Kuwaiti institutions work to help people in need, not only at home but also around the world. One of the oldest and most active institutions is the Kuwait Red Crescent Society (KRCS). Established in 1966, this organisation sends food, medicine, and aid to families affected by natural disasters. Its volunteers have operated in several countries, giving shelter, healthcare, and clean water to those in difficult situations. The KRCS also supports hospitals and schools in Kuwait and coaches young people on how to become responsible volunteers that people can count on.

Another important organisation is the Direct Aid Society, founded in 1981. It focuses on long-term development instead of short-term help. Direct Aid constructs schools and hospitals, provides clean water, and supports families in Africa and Asia. Its volunteers believe that education and health are the keys to strong communities. The group also runs training programmes to help many women acquire new skills and earn an income independently to support their families.

A third well-known centre is the Kuwait Voluntary Work Centre (KVWC), established in 1974. It encourages young Kuwaitis to serve their community. The centre organises activities such as cleaning beaches, planting trees, and visiting hospitals. Members learn about teamwork, leadership, and environmental care. The KVWC has created a new generation of active citizens who see volunteering as a duty and a joy. Together, these institutions show that helping others is a blessing and is at the heart of Kuwait's true progress.

Lesson 6

Verbs Followed by a Gerund (verb + -ing)

Grammar · Activities as nouns

📘 The Rule

A gerund is a verb + -ing used as a noun to talk about an activity (swimming, reading, playing…).

Some verbs are always followed by a gerund:

enjoy finish practise suggest discuss encourage avoid consider
  • He enjoyed painting the walls.
  • They finished watering all the flowers.
  • The student practised explaining the lesson to the class.
  • My sister suggested volunteering at the cooperative society.
  • We discussed helping families in need with charity.
  • The teacher encouraged serving our community.
